## Verdict

**The machine never halts.** It is a tetration-rate counter built from "levels":
- Within a level, each (01) pair of a block A pays for one x4 transfer of a counter n.
- At the end of a level, the counter becomes the next level's block.
- Unlike the related `1RB0RF_1RC0LF...` machine, no residue analysis is needed. Only parities matter, and they are
  forced: every rule's output has exactly the parities the next rule needs.

There is no sigma, and it is **not a record candidate**.

## Configuration families

Tape order, blank outside the word; `X>b` means the head is in state X reading the cell b.

```
Lv(N)    = E>1 (10)^N 1
be(A, n) = A>1 (01)^A 1010110111 (10)^n 1
al(A, n) = A>1 (01)^A 1010110101 (01)^n
X(m)     =     A>1 010110111 (10)^m 1        (the be word without its leading 1)
```

These "event" forms are the moments the head reaches the left end of the word. They occur in the middle of a
leftward sweep (states E, A, F, D cycling), and a crossing lemma skips over them. The Coq proof therefore uses the
configurations 4 steps later, which have the same parameters:

```
beX(A, n) = A>0 (01)^(A+1) 1010110111 (10)^n 1          (Coq beX)
alX(A, n) = A>0 (01)^(A+1) 1 (01)^2 1 (01)^(n+2)        (Coq alX)
Xc(m)     = A>0 (01)^3 10111 (10)^m 1                   (Coq Xc)
Lv(N)     = E>1 (10)^N 1                                (Coq Lv, unchanged)
```

**Literal checks** (`node check_rules.js 1`, output in `check_rules_out.txt`, step counts in `check_rules_steps.json`):
- Every run starts from the exact word on a blank tape.
- A run passes only if it reaches exactly the predicted configuration (full tape, state and head position) with no

**Parities** (`check_par.js`, `check_par_out.txt`): the conditions in the table are needed. BE with n odd or A
even, AL with n even or A even, X with m odd, and LvE with N odd all fail to reach the stated target. The trajectory
never produces those parities (next section).

**Mechanism.**
- **Transfer loop** (Coq `T1`, `TL`, `TL2`). In stream order next to the head:
  `0 >> [1;0]^^b *> [0;1]^^(m+1) *> l {{B}}> 0^inf` goes to `0 >> [1;0]^^(b+4) *> [0;1]^^m *> l {{B}}> 0^inf`.
  In tape order, `l (10)^(m+1) (01)^b 0 B>` becomes `l (10)^m (01)^(b+4) 0 B>`. Each pass sweeps left across the
  destination (states E, A, F, D, two cells per 4 steps) and back right (states B, C). So each pass costs Θ(counter)
  steps, and a whole transfer is quadratic.
- Each of BE, AL and LvO runs one transfer whose source is the counter. The concrete cells around it add the
  constants.
- The block (01)^A is swept a few times per rule by crossing lemmas and loses one (01) pair per rule.
- LvE and X rebuild the level structure at the left end.

## Levels and the recurrence

A level starts at be(A, 10) with A ≡ 3 (mod 4). BE and AL alternate:

    be(A, n) -> al(A-2, 4n+15) -> be(A-4, 16n+80)

Parities stay forced. be always has n even (10, then 16n+80), and al always has n odd (4n+15). A stays odd and
decreases by 2 per rule. After (A-3)/4 double steps the level reaches be(3, n_end) with

    n_end = (46·16^((A-3)/4) - 16) / 3.

The level ending is then forced:

    be(3, n) -> al(1, 4n+15) -> X(16n+80) -> Lv(64n+335) -> Lv(256n+1346) -> be(256n+1343, 10)

Here 4n+15 is odd (AL1 applies), 16n+80 is even and ≥ 4 (X applies), and 64n+335 is odd (LvO applies).
256n+1346 is even (LvE applies), and 256n+1343 ≡ 3 (mod 4), so the next level has the same shape. No step ever
needs a parity outside the rule table, so no level ending reaches H0. This is the Coq invariant
`Cf (i, n) = beX(4i+3, 2n+4)` with `level_step`.

## Pipeline growth data

- Quick_Sim sees the ×4 transfers, which later combine into ×16 double steps, and applies each proven rule at most
  10 times per loop. The rising digits per loop is the rate of rule application, not super-tetration growth.
- Level 2 ends with an 18,900-digit counter, and level 3's block has 18,903 digits. The pipeline's 22,691 digits
  at 10 s and 48,122 at 40 s are therefore both inside level 3: the level transition came before 10 s.
- The final Quick_Sim tape at loop 9808 (`qs9808.txt`, 43 s) is `0101^(~10^^2.6) 1010 1110 0101^(~10^^2.7) (0101) C>`.
  That is the level-3 block (about 18,900 digits) and a 48,123-digit counter. So the digits-per-loop jump between
  10 s and 40 s happens inside level 3, from Quick_Sim's rule-application rate, and is not a new kind of growth.
- Quick_Sim trace `qs2000.txt` shows:

  - the x4 transfer toward Lv(62786) (loop 1076: `1010^31392`, i.e. about (10)^62784);

## Record comparison and variants

- The machine never halts, so it has no score and does not bear on the BB(8) bar 2↑¹¹2↑¹¹3.
- It has the same level architecture as `1RB0RF_1RC0LF_0RD0RB_1LE1LG_0LA0LC_0LD1RH_1RA1LC_1RF---` (non-halting,
  mod-55 residue cycle) and `1RB0RF_1RC1RH_...` (halts at level 2). It is not a trivial variant of either: seven
  transitions differ from `1RB0RF_1RC0LF...`, and the block, the rate (×4 per (01) pair) and the level map all
  differ. Here the level map avoids halting by parity alone, not by a residue cycle.
- All 15 defined transitions are used in the first 10^8 steps (`usage_out.txt`). H0 is never reached, as the proof
  shows, so every completion of H0 gives the same non-halting machine.
