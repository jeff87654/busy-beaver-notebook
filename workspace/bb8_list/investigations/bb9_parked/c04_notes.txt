8. Why the halt is not residue-decidable
   - The level-1 cascade ends only when the near list is SMALL; its small values are (3^S/5^T)*B + c, i.e. they depend
     on the LEADING digits of B (B = (arrow(13,2,3)-4)/2), not on residues.  The endgame there decides between a
     further residue stop, an R1 route, a parked-list activation (escalation) or (in some contexts) H1.
   - After an escalation the next level's cascade needs ~ log(12 R2) exhaustions, which is >> every B-scale counter
     (-3 per exhaustion), so the counters run out mid-cascade and the reload / digit-decrement / halt rules come into
     play.  Those happen only at sizes no simulation can reach.  The old B=6 / g6 / small worlds that "halted at H1"
     had R3 > R2 and B-scale counters ~60, so their halts are artefacts.
   - Conclusion: C04 halts iff a nested counter/cascade process on astronomically large, specific integers ends in an
     H1 endgame.  No proof route; likely f_(w+1)-shape if it never halts, >= f_w(f_w(12B)) if it halts after level 2.
