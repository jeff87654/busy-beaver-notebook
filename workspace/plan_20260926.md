# BB(8) search: status, measurements and plan — 2026-09-26 (evening)

Artifacts for this note are in `estimate_20260926/`.

## How the BB(7) champion was found (sources: bbchallenge wiki BB(7) page, machine page, busy-beaver-discuss)

- 2025-05-06: mxdys released the TNF enumerator (github.com/ccz181078/TM). The tree is split into 10^6 subtasks, and the deciders run inline. Every machine that halts within 300,000 steps is discarded, so any huge halter leaves the enumerator as a *holdout*.
- Within about three days, Shawn and Terry Ligocki ran their deciders over enumerator output and found three tetrational BB(7) champions (~10↑↑22, 10↑↑35, 10↑↑46).
- 2025-05-10: Pavel Kropitz found `1RB0RA_1LC1LF_1RD0LB_1RA1LE_1RZ0LC_1RG1LD_0RG0RF` after "enumerating only a small portion of the BB(7) domain". It lies in subtask 243308.
- 2025-05-13: Shawn Ligocki analysed it. The rules are B(a; b+1, …) → B(2a+4; b, …) and B(a; 0^k, 0, n+1, …) → B(0; 0^k, a+2, n, …). The machine halts from B(a; 0^k), giving σ between 2↑¹¹2↑¹¹3 and 2↑¹¹2↑¹¹4.
- Phase 1, the full enumeration, ran May–Jul 2025 with 14 contributors and left ~86M holdouts. Phase 2 cut these to 15,743,264 by June 2026, using about 93k core-hours. Nothing has beaten the champion.

## BB(8) community status

- The wiki BB(8) page (edited 2026-03-19) says: "unsolved and largely unexplored". Its champion is the BB(7) champion by default.
- Nov 2025 test: one of the 10^6 subtasks took 7.5 h and left 35,000 holdouts. The page projects about 1.47×10^11 holdouts in total.
- No published genuine 8-state search exists, and the BB(8) row on the Champions page is blank.

## New measurements (this session)

1. **End-to-end regression passed.** The 7-state control of BB(7) subtask 243308 was resumed to completion:
   - 15,120,490 nodes, 157 holdouts, about 25 CPU-min on this PC under full load (12 workers, 2¼ min wall).
   - The champion is holdout #88 in `estimate_20260926/bb7_job243308_full/holdouts_243308_all.txt`.
   - So the local pipeline reproduces the historical discovery step.
2. **Tree-size estimator** (`make_knuth.py` → `knuth_tnf.cpp`, built with `-DBBN=n`):
   - It is Knuth's random-probe estimator wrapped around mxdys's unmodified deciders.
   - Validation: for 4 states it estimates 619k nodes against an exact 620,261. For the champion's 12-transition prefix it estimates 7,963 nodes and 175 holdouts against an exact 7,937 and 167.

   Full-tree estimates:

   | States | TNF nodes | Holdouts (noisy) | CPU on this PC |
   | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
   | 5 | 1.27e8 | ~0 | 1.4 h |
   | 6 | 3.35e10 (wiki: ~33.4B) | ~5e5 | 27 days |
   | 7 | 1.10e13 (1.1e7/subtask) | 4e8 (actual: 8.6e7) | 47 years |
   | 8 | **4.3e15 (±1%)** | ~1e11–6e11 | **~40,000 years** |

   - From 7 to 8 states, nodes grow about 390× and CPU about 870×.
   - This PC runs about 8–10× slower per subtask than the wiki's "~2 min" figure, so the absolute years are pessimistic.
   - Even on community hardware, BB(8) would take about 10^3 core-years plus a triage of more than 10^11 holdouts. **A full enumeration is out of reach.**

4. **Structural point.** In the 8-state tree, the champion with E0 undefined is a single holdout (m=13 in the table), because simulation never reaches E0. Its halt-side extensions (E0→H, with H0/H1 free) are therefore **never generated** by brute-force enumeration. They need symbolic continuation.

## Revised plan (after review: build a BB(8) candidate list)

Why the plan changed:
- All four 2025 BB(7) champions came fresh from enumeration, not from modifying a smaller champion.
- No public BB(8) holdout or candidate list exists:
  - the wiki's Nov 2025 sample (one subtask: 7.5 h, 35k holdouts) is cited only via Discord;
  - the Holdouts-lists page stops at 7 states;
  - the Champions page's BB(8) row is blank;
  - GitHub has no BB8 config of mxdys's code.
- Pascal Michel's survey records "Racheline"'s 2024 claim of S(8,2) > 2↑^5 4. That is far below the BB(7) champion.

Calibration of the growth ranking (`estimate_20260926/rank_growth.py`):
- Method: Quick_Sim `--recursive --max-loops 2000`, then rank by the decimal digits of the ones count reached. Environment: `third_party/venv311`.
- BB(7) job 243308 (157 holdouts): the real champion is **#1** with 1,209 digits (4 s). The next is 37.
- 12 random BB(7) jobs (`bb7_random_jobs/`; 1,509 holdouts; mean 1.23e7 nodes, 126 holdouts, 974 s per job here): exactly one outlier, `1RB1LD_1RC0RF_0LA0RA_0LA0LE_1LA0LG_0LA---_1RD1LG` (1,248 digits; ~10^8,940 at 5,000 loops; status unknown). Everything else is at most 40 digits.
- So the ranking flags roughly 1 per 1,000 holdouts and caught the known champion. That is one positive example, not a proof of sensitivity.
- The default triage used earlier (100k loops / 30 s timeout) just left the champion as one of 67 anonymous timeouts.
- BB(7) density (wiki / jhuang97 releases):
  - every 1% slice had dozens of machines above 10↑↑5 and roughly 10–15 above 10↑↑15;
  - the only Ackermann-level machine is the champion, in one 1% slice.

Plan:
1. **Ask the community first** (bbchallenge Discord). Is the Nov 2025 BB(8) sample file available? Is anyone else planning BB(8)? Use the same subtask IDs and a documented `#define BB8` block, as the BB(2,7) effort did.
2. **Enumerate random BB(8) subtasks with the unmodified enumerator.**
   - Estimated cost here: ~60 CPU-h per subtask (7.5 h community figure × ~8 slowdown measured on BB(7)). Split each subtask across cores with the frontier resume, as done for job 243308.
   - The first subtask doubles as a timing and holdout-count measurement.
3. **Growth-rank every holdout** (~5 CPU-s each), then deep-dive the outliers:
   - longer Quick_Sim runs;
   - rule inspection and a hand-proof of the recurrence;
   - check halting versus merely fast-growing nonhalting.
   - Throughput is about 3 subtasks (~100k holdouts) per day on 16 cores; ranking costs about as much as enumeration.
4. **Publish** the holdouts, rankings and code hash (e.g. as a GitHub release, like jhuang97/bb7x2) so others can extend the list.
5. **Side experiments, lower priority now:** champion halt-extensions and neighborhoods (below). Proving the 5 remaining small pilot candidates is lowest priority.

## Earlier plan (champion-centred; now secondary)

1. **Halt-side extensions of the champion.** This is cheap and gives the best odds of a real improvement.
   - About 128 nontrivial machines: E0 ∈ {0,1}×{L,R}→H (4 choices), then 32 choices for the H transition read first (the tape fixes which symbol), with the other H transition left as the halt.
   - For each, start from concrete small B(a; 0^k) (a ≤ ~20, k ≤ 12). Replace the halt with the variant, run the variant and recognize the next B-form, reusing the recognizer in `theory_init_20260926.py`.
   - Look for maps that re-enter the recurrence with a nonzero vector, e.g. B(a; 0^k) → B(0; 0^(k-1), f(a)). Such a map would iterate the whole 2↑¹¹ function and reach about 2↑¹²-level.
   - Prove any candidate rule by the same universal sweep lemmas used for C07/C10, then compute the bound.
2. **Champion neighborhoods, m = 12 → 8** (under 1 h of enumeration in total).
   - Enumerate with `knuth8.exe exactfrom <prefix> <a> <b> <c> <holdouts.txt>`, using the prefixes in `champion_neighborhood/path8.txt`, split across cores.
   - Triage the holdouts in this order:
     1. mxdys `exec` deciders (pull current upstream first: the FAR/DFA deciders were released January 2026, and the local copy is pinned older);
     2. Ligocki `Enumerate.py`/`Quick_Sim` at BB(7) Phase-2 settings (`--block-mult=5..21 --max-loops=250k..1M`);
     3. a champion B-form recognizer, applied to anything still unresolved.
   - Feed holdouts that have ≥2 undefined transitions back through TNF expansion, not 1RZ completion.
   - Then repeat the same for the other known big BB(7) machines (the three tetrational champions) and for Wythagoras-style extensions of the BB(6) champion.
3. **Go to m = 7 (about 11 h) only if step 2's triage throughput is acceptable.** The main cost is triage, not enumeration.
4. **Low priority.**
   - Random full subtasks (each ≈ 7.5 h on community hardware and 35k holdouts): this is a lottery.
   - Proving the 5 remaining pilot candidates (C03/C13/C15 classes) nonhalting: these are small machines unrelated to the champion, and settling them cannot produce a record.
5. **Prerequisite.** Rebuild a clean Python environment for Ligocki's tools. `third_party/runtime_deps/gmpy2` is permission-restricted, and the earlier runs used a bundled 3.12 interpreter.
