Each primitive is entered at its own entry state (a TM state cannot be parameterised, so per-op
descents are genuine duplicates and are counted honestly). All ops share the return scanners
`ret.adv.*` / `ret.zero.*` (D-side) in `frag_shared.tm`.

## Honest assessment vs the <= 100-state target

The implemented data side is **160 distinct states**, already over 100, dominated by **RB (43)**,
**PUSH (32)** and **CHECK (28)**. Reasons:

* **CHECK (28)** must count exact run lengths 1,3,8,8; counting to 8 twice cannot be shared (a state
  is not parameterisable), so ~20 states are unavoidable in a 2-symbol machine without a mark symbol.
* **RB (43)** carries three outcomes (adv/zero/END) plus the full N binary-queue rotation (two bit
  paths that cannot share the write, and a destructive END with compaction).
* **PUSH (32)**: SM_SPEC's "chain of 9" is only 9 states when the work wff sits at the free end with
  nothing beyond it. With `c` pinned at the IP-side (the layout that makes INCC/DECC/NEW*/APP1 cheap),
  PUSH must shuttle, costing ~23 extra shared states (the 9 are only the entry chain).

The four unimplemented primitives (DUP, DUPT, DET, COMMIT) all need the SM_SPEC **STEP-L / STEP-R**
shuttle engine (move a wff across the A|B boundary one unit at a time, carrying a run-boundary flag),
plus compaction to preserve the "no 0-run > 2 inside D" invariant. That engine is the single missing
piece; once built (~20 shared states), DUPT/DUP/DET/COMMIT layer on top with the estimates above.

Reaching <= 100 overall would require the free-end-growth layout (cheap 9-state PUSH, no per-op
`c`-shift) and sharing the STEP engine across DUP/DUPT/DET/COMMIT/CHECK — plausible at roughly
100-130 states for the full data side, but not at 100 with RB's N-rotation implemented inline.
