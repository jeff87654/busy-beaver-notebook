  Verification by literal simulation (src/vchamp3.py, worlds_k3.py, haltcfg.exe):
    #1 champion 1RB1RA_1RC1RZ_1LD0RF_1RA0LE_0LD1RC_1RA0RE (mxdys, 10^^10^^10^^8.1, between 2^^^5 and 2^^^6):
       the exact tape of S1(3,7,2,6,63) (wiki start config) is reached at step 27965 (state E, head on the last cell of
       LC(a,0)). Rst0 holds: every one of 450 grid configs S1(len0,0,0,a,b) halts at B1 within 70-160 steps.

       C(4k) halts at D1 for k=1..6.
    #4-#20: each table runs from the blank tape, and its natural configurations halt at its single 1RZ slot (generic
       worlds below). They were not checked against a stated number.
  Engines: all are fixed depth. The champion has one binary counter LC, a mod-3 counter m, and two unary values a, b
    (each Inc does b -> 2^b-1, each Rst1 adds one 2^^). Rounds are decided by mod-3 luck. #2 and #3 are exponential
    Collatz maps with 1/3 or 1/4 halting odds per step, and the rest are 10^^x or below. None has a list whose length
    sets the arrow level, which is what M8 needed: the old BB(7) champion has 11 levels.
  Construction (src/enum7.py): halt slot -> {0,1}{L,R}G, G0/G1 = halt or {0,1}{L,R}{A..G}, at least one halt.
    That gives 4 x 57 = 228 machines per base and 4,560 in all. G is only reachable through the redirect, so there
    are no TNF duplicates.
  Worlds (pre-halt configurations, run literally to their real halt):
    rule worlds: champion 450 S1(len0<=6, 0, 0, a<=14, b in {0,1,3,7,15}); #2: 82 (H3 grid + natural H1 halts);
    #3: C(4k), k=1..6.
    generic worlds (all 20): 24 blank-tape snapshots up to 1e8 steps. Long blocks are shrunk to small sizes, keeping
    residues mod 4 or 6 (3 variants) or randomizing them (21 variants). The shrunk configs are run at most 3e6 steps,
    and the halting configurations are kept: 5-439 per base (wg/).
    At the halt the head always sees the same local window in every world of a base (e.g. champion 01001011[1]1...).
    So G's first read is fixed and does not depend on the parameters.
  Follow: ext.exe, literal, 2e6 steps per (machine, world). Episodes = uses of the redirected slot more than 4 world
    lengths apart. The 89 LOOP machines of the champion were re-run 2e7 steps on 42 worlds with b <= 1, where rounds
    stay feasible.

  LOOP machines (b1, looked at by tape dumps): the redirect leaves an S1-form tape (LH LC.. prefix, growing b block),
    and the base engine resumes a new round. At later Rst0 events G sees the same local window, so its decision
    repeats: every episode either loops or repeats the non-halting branch. 4 machines are in-place loops (ones ratio 1,
    3e5-1e6 episodes). At 2e7 steps with b <= 1, none of the 89 LOOP machines halted after a rebuild.
  Size estimates for halters: IMM and SOON give the base score plus at most x1.2, or plus a fixed factor.
    REHALT means the base engine ran one or two more rounds and the gadget then halted. Small-world ratios are 3-1051.
    At the real scale that is at most one extra iteration of the base's step map: 10^^(x+1) for #2-#20, and one more
    2^^ round, about 2^^^6, for the champion. Even unboundedly many rounds of these engines stay below hexation.
    f_omega(20) needs about 20 arrows.
  Why it fails (cf. bb8_c231ext): the M8 effect needs a base whose exhaustion leaves a huge accumulator AND an engine
    whose depth is set by a list length that the gadget can rebuild from that accumulator. No published 6-state halter
    has such an engine, and no 6-state holdout shows one (dlscan2 zero, evo no level lists). One extra state can add
    rounds of a fixed-depth engine, not levels.

CONTROL (positive): M8 REPRODUCED: YES
  The same enumerator (enum7.py) applied to the old BB(7) champion's E0 gives 260 8-state rows. This is exactly
  collapse_search's set8. The rows were run in cfollow3.exe's champion jump world (R=66, 1e9 steps; o_m8ctl_R66.txt):
    halters with 1 escalation 165, with 2 escalations 8, with >= 3 escalations 0.
    The 2-escalation rows are M8 = ..._1LH0LC_..._1RG--- (Ks 11 -> 817, 3284 ones in this world), its twins 0RB---,
    0RE--- (1LH) and 0RC---, 1RD--- (0LH) with 3283-3284 ones, plus 3 small rows (Ks 11 -> 12).
    This matches collapse_search/notes.txt (set8 world R=66).
  Caveat: the 6-state bases were followed by the literal ext.exe, because the jump world is specific to the champion
    engine. A literal follower would show M8 as a LOOP (its 2nd round is f_omega(b)), so LOOP rows are the place an
    M8-type machine would hide. That is why the LOOP rows were inspected (above). Their resumed engine is the
    fixed-depth base engine, not a rebuilt list.
